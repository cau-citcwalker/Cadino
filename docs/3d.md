# 3D in Cadino

> **Last updated:** 2026-10-24 · **Status:** in progress (construction planes, gumball, 3D snaps, STRETCH and grip edits with heights; face and edge editing, PRESSPULL, sections, FLATSHOT, INTERFERE, polysolids, conversions, meshes and Hidden; perspective views, DVIEW with TWist and CLip, the 3D ViewCube and RENDER on a worker landed) · **Describes:** `main`

The user guide and design reference for Cadino's 3D: what it is for, how it is driven, every 3D
command, how the pieces fit, how 3D data is stored, and the known ceilings. Cadino is CAD-rhino,
built on the CADCraft 2D core.

## What Cadino is for

Cadino is for furniture designers. A drawing that came from AutoCAD behaves exactly as it does in
AutoCAD in 2D, in World and under any level UCS. The 3D views edit the model the way Rhino does.
Shop drawings, cut lists and a model that Rhino can open all come out of the same drawing.

| Step | What you do | Commands |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Draw | Draw plans and elevations in 2D with AutoCAD's controls | LINE, PLINE, RECTANG, CIRCLE, OFFSET, TRIM … |
| 2. Model | Turn closed profiles into boards and parts | PANEL, EXTRUDE, REVOLVE, LOFT, SWEEP, PLANESURF, BOX … |
| 3. Edit in 3D | Arrange and shape parts in Rhino-style views | gumball, 3DMOVE, 3DROTATE, 3DALIGN, 3DARRAY, MIRROR3D, UNION, SUBTRACT, FILLETEDGE, CHAMFEREDGE, SOLIDEDIT, PRESSPULL, SHELL, SLICE |
| 4. Name and check parts | Give each solid a part name and a material; find parts that overlap | PART (PANEL takes `name` and `material` too), INTERFERE |
| 5. Document | Lay out drawing views, sections and flat views, and list the parts | VIEWBASE (Make2D) on a layout, SECTION, SECTIONPLANE, FLATSHOT, CUTLIST as a table or CSV |
| 6. Exchange | Send the model to Rhino and bring it back | RHINOOUT, IGESOUT, STEPOUT, STLOUT; IGESIN, STEPIN |

## Controls

A view looking straight down world Z is a **plan view** and works as AutoCAD's 2D drawing area.
Every other view direction is a **3D view** and works the way Rhino's views do. 3D views exist on
the Model tab only. Layouts and MSPACE viewports show plan.

### Plan views: AutoCAD

All of this stays AutoCAD's in a plan view, whatever the construction plane:

- The command line, aliases, prompts, keywords, transparent commands and scripts.
- Object snaps, tracking, polar and ortho, grid and snap (isometric too), and Dynamic Input.
- Picking with the pick box, window selection (drag left to right) and crossing selection (right
  to left), PICKFIRST, and grips with their menus.
- The wheel zooms about the cursor, a middle drag pans, and a middle double-click zooms to the
  extents. Shift+middle drag orbits, as AutoCAD's transparent 3DORBIT does. On macOS a trackpad
  pans with two fingers and zooms with a pinch.
- While a command runs, a right click is Enter (held, it opens the command menu). With no command
  running it opens the shortcut menu. Shift+right-click or Ctrl+right-click opens the object snap
  menu.
- The 2D UCS icon.
- A UCS moved or turned about Z (a **level** UCS) keeps all of the above; points arrive in its
  coordinates.

There is no gumball in plan views.

### 3D views: Rhino

| Input | What it does |
|---|---|
| Right drag | Orbits about the point at the centre of the screen, as deep as the middle of the model (a perspective view: about its target). The elevation stops just short of straight up or down |
| Shift+right drag | Pans (in perspective the eye and the target move together) |
| Ctrl+right drag | Zooms (dragging up zooms in; in perspective the eye dollies toward the target) |
| Right click without moving | Stays AutoCAD's: Enter, or a shortcut menu |
| Middle drag, Shift+middle drag | Pans; orbits |
| Wheel | Zooms about the cursor (in perspective the eye dollies toward the point under the cursor) |
| Click, window, crossing | Pick what is drawn (lines and shaded faces) instead of the plan shadow |
| ViewCube | A shaded cube turned as the view is (perspective and twisted views too), each face labelled. A click on a face's middle looks straight at that face (the top is PLAN), on an edge along the edge between its two faces, on a corner from that corner (the south-west top one is the SW isometric); the part under the pointer is highlighted with a tooltip. Home is the south-west isometric. Dragging the compass ring at the cube's foot orbits. The WCS pill opens the construction-plane menu |
| UCS icon | Drawn in 3D. A click opens the same menu: World (WCS) and CPlane Front, Right, Back, Left or Bottom |
| Grid | The construction plane's grid (GRIDMODE, GRIDUNIT, GRIDMAJOR) with its x axis red and its y axis green |
| Points | A picked point lands where the view ray meets the tile's construction plane. A view that sees the plane edge-on says so in the status bar |
| 3D snaps | END, MID and CEN on solid edges (CEN on circular edges). END, MID, CEN and QUA on curves (lines, polylines, 3D polylines, arcs, circles, ellipses, splines), where they are in 3D. The snapped height goes to the command |
| Grips | Objects show their grips where they are in 3D. Solids show none: use the gumball |
| Gumball | Appears with a selection and no command running. Arrows move along world X (red), Y (green) and Z (blue), arcs rotate about those axes, and the centre square scales. A number typed during a drag sets the exact distance, angle (degrees) or factor. Letting go runs 3DMOVE, 3DROTATE or 3DSCALE as one undo step. Esc cancels. A grip under a handle wins. View > Gumball and the status bar toggle it (`ui.gumball`) |

### Viewports

| Action | Result |
|---|---|
| VIEWPORTS.RHINO (Rhino's `4View`) | Four tiles, each zoomed to the extents: Top (current, World plane), Perspective (a true perspective from the south-west through a 50 mm lens, World plane), Front (Front plane) and Right (Right plane) |
| VIEWPORTS.SINGLE, VIEWPORTS.MAXIMIZE (`MaxViewport`) | Show the current tile alone, then bring the others back. MAXIMIZE in one view with nothing remembered lays out the four Rhino views |
| Click in another tile | Makes that tile current. In a 3D tile the click also picks there; in a plan tile it only makes the tile current |
| Right drag starting in another 3D tile | Makes that tile current and navigates it |
| Tile title (`+ \| view \| style`) | A click (either button) opens a menu: the preset views, Wireframe, Hidden, Shaded and Shaded with Edges, Parallel or Perspective, Maximize or Restore Viewports, and Four Views. A double-click maximizes the tile or restores the others. A perspective tile's title says Perspective |

Each tile has its own view, view direction, visual style, projection and construction plane. The
tiles are AutoCAD's model space VPORTS, so -VPORTS, VPORTS and View > Viewports work on them too.
Arranging tiles is an undo step. View commands (VPOINT, PLAN, the presets, 3DORBIT, VSCURRENT,
PERSPECTIVE, DVIEW) act on the current tile, and ZOOM Previous keeps a history for each tile.

### Perspective views

A perspective view looks through a camera, as Rhino's Perspective view and AutoCAD's DVIEW
Distance do: nearer is larger, parallel edges meet toward the horizon.

- PERSPECTIVE 1 (typed, SETVAR, or Perspective in the tile title's menu) turns the current view's
  perspective on; 0 (Parallel) turns it off. Turning it on keeps what is on screen at the
  middle of the model: the camera's target plane goes through it, the eye on the line of sight
  through the centre of the view. VIEWPORTS.RHINO's Perspective tile is a perspective from the
  start. PLAN and the Top view turn it off, as AutoCAD's PLAN does: plan is the 2D drawing area.
- The lens is a 35 mm camera's (its 24 mm frame height across the view's height): 50 mm, about
  27° high, by default. LENSLENGTH reports it; DVIEW Zoom changes it (the eye stays, the view
  widens or narrows).
- Zooming dollies the eye toward the target plane (the wheel: toward the point under the cursor),
  panning moves the eye and the target together, orbiting turns about the target, and ZOOM
  Extents (and a new direction from VPOINT or a preset) backs the eye off until every corner of
  the model's box is in view (its height as drawn: what block references hold counts too). ZOOM
  Window frames the window's corners on the XY plane the same way, and its rubber band runs
  through the corners under the cursor. ZOOM Previous brings back the camera with the view.
- Picks, windows, snaps, grips and the gumball work as in any 3D view, through the eye: a picked
  point is where the ray from the eye meets the construction plane. Nothing behind the eye draws;
  what comes nearer than a hundredth of the distance to the target is cut away, and neither snaps
  nor shows grips (a line running past the eye is hit-tested where it is drawn).
- The camera is saved with each tile (DXF VPORT: VIEWMODE 71 bit 1, lens 42, the target 17 and
  the camera along 16), so a drawing opens with its perspective tiles; one from AutoCAD opens
  with its perspective view.
- DVIEW TWist turns the picture about the line of sight (counter-clockwise). DVIEW CLip sets a
  front and a back clipping plane square to the line of sight, as distances from the target
  toward the camera (the front one can be at the Eye): nothing nearer than the front plane or
  farther than the back one draws, picks or snaps, and RENDER leaves it out; a pair with the
  front behind the back sees nothing. Both work in parallel and perspective views; a twisted or
  clipped plan view counts as a 3D view. Moving the camera keeps them; VPOINT, PLAN, the preset
  views and orbiting give an upright view without planes. Each tile keeps them (DXF VPORT twist
  51, front and back 43 and 44, VIEWMODE bits 2, 4 and 16).

### Rendering

RENDER (`rr`) renders the current Model view, perspective or parallel, into the Render window:
the solids and meshes shaded in their parts' materials' colours (PART, PANEL `material`; a solid
with no material in its own colour), smooth across curved faces and sharp at creases, lit by a
key light from above and to the left of the viewer, a fill light and ambient light, with a little
sheen, over a soft studio background. The window offers the view's size on screen or a fixed
size up to 3840 × 2160, edges on or off, Render again, and Save Image... (a PNG). It renders on
a worker thread from a snapshot of the drawing and the view, so the app goes on meanwhile: the
window shows a spinner with the time taken and Cancel, which stops the render. Render again (or
closing the window) stops the one running, and one render runs at a time. An XCLIPped block
reference's solids are cut at its clip boundary and its Clipdepth planes. It is a quick
look at the design, not a photoreal render: there are no shadows, reflections, textures,
transparency, or lights of the drawing's own.

Materials are read by name, English or Korean: walnut, wenge, mahogany, cherry, teak, oak, ash,
beech, maple, birch, pine, bamboo, plywood, MDF, particle board, glass, mirror, stainless steel or
aluminium, steel, brass, copper, marble or stone, concrete, leather, fabric, and black, white
(melamine, LPM, HPL), grey, red, blue, green or yellow. Any other name gets a muted colour of its
own, the same each time.

### Construction planes (CPLANE, UCS)

- CPLANE (Rhino) and UCS (AutoCAD) set the current tile's construction plane. In one view they set
  the drawing's plane. Setting a plane is an undo step.
- Presets: World (or Top), Front, Right, Back, Left and Bottom, all at the world origin, as
  AutoCAD's orthographic UCSs are. Front has x = X, y = Z and normal −Y.
- At the prompt: `Specify origin of UCS or [World/Top/Front/Right/BAck/Left/BOttom/3point/X/Y/Z]
  <World>`, then a point on the X axis and a point on the XY plane (Enter keeps the current
  axes). X, Y and Z turn the plane about its own axis (Enter takes 90°), as AutoCAD's
  `UCS Z 90` does.
- Commands work in the plane. Points given to a command are plane coordinates, and objects it
  creates are placed in the plane. MOVE, COPY, ROTATE, SCALE, MIRROR and STRETCH act in 3D through
  the plane. Picks, snaps and grips see the drawing in plane coordinates. An object that can't be
  expressed in a tilted plane (dimensions and other objects that stay level) can't be picked there.
- Plan-only commands (OFFSET, TRIM, EXTEND, FILLET, CHAMFER, HATCH, most dimensions and dimension
  edits (DIMTEDIT, DIMJOGLINE), multileader edits, the geometric and dimensional constraints,
  ARRAY, BLOCK, COPYBASE, PASTECLIP and others: `PLAN_ONLY` in `crates/engine/src/cplane.rs`) run
  in World when the plane is level (moved and/or turned about Z, AutoCAD's everyday 2D UCS), with
  their points converted; their JSON forms then take world points. A tilted plane refuses them
  with a message. A 2D session under `UCS 100,50` and `UCS Z 90` gives the same world results as
  in World (tested in `crates/engine/tests/level_ucs_session.rs`).
- DIMORDINATE measures from the UCS origin along the axes of a UCS turned by any angle about Z.
  The dimension keeps the UCS's x direction (DXF group 51), so it measures the same after saving,
  reopening or ROTATE.
- Selection windows select what the screen rectangle shows, also in a turned UCS. The JSON
  `select {window}` takes UCS corners and selects inside that UCS-aligned rectangle.
- LIMITS takes UCS corners and stores the world box through them (LIMMIN and LIMMAX are world
  points, as in AutoCAD). In a tilted plane (the Front tile) the corners are taken as world x, y.
- STRETCH and grip edits take a height as MOVE does: JSON `delta: [dx, dy, dz]` (or `from`/`to`
  with heights), typed `@0,0,100` or `x,y,z`, or a 3D snap. Line ends and the vertices of 3D
  polylines and 3D faces move in Z, and what moves whole (an object wholly inside the window, a
  point, an arc or circle by its centre, a grip that moves its whole object) moves in 3D. STRETCH
  refuses a height for a planar object only partly inside, and changes nothing then. A grip that
  can't leave its object's plane (a polyline's vertex, an arc's end) takes the point into that
  plane, so a snap to a point of the object's own plane, or at another height, still stretches
  it. STRETCH doesn't stretch 3D solids: one wholly inside the window moves. A grip edit's
  `@dx,dy,dz` is measured from the base grip; grip ROTATE, SCALE and MIRROR stay in the plane.
- A drawing from AutoCAD or another program opens in World in every tile, so its 2D work behaves
  as it did there.
- Layouts, MSPACE viewports and the Block Editor always work in World.

### Typed coordinates and the status bar

| Typed | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `x,y` | A point on the construction plane |
| `x,y,z` | z is the height along the plane's normal. Commands that build in 3D (BOX, CYLINDER …) start there |
| `@dx,dy,dz` | Relative to the last point, its height included. `@dx,dy` and `@d<a` keep the last height |
| `#x,y` | Absolute |

The status bar shows the cursor's x and y in the construction plane, and the height of a 3D snap.

## Commands

Every command is reachable from the command line, the menus, scripts, the control channel and MCP.
The JSON forms below are what `engine.execute` runs. No dialogs open.

Some rules apply to all of them:

- Sizes under 1e-6 drawing units are refused.
- Objects on locked layers are left alone.
- A command that fails leaves the drawing as it was.
- A boolean's result replaces the first solid, keeping its properties, and erases the others.
- As AutoCAD's default DELOBJ does, curves that make a solid are erased and curves that make a
  surface stay.
- A surface is a 3D solid with an open mesh.
- `handles?` means: the handles given, else the selection.

### Where JSON points are

| Points are in… | Commands |
|---|---|
| The current construction plane (World: the world) | 2D drawing commands, 3DPOLY, HELIX, the primitives (BOX, CYLINDER, CONE, SPHERE, TORUS, WEDGE, PYRAMID), PLANESURF and PANEL by corners (PANEL's `at` too) |
| The world | 3DMOVE, 3DROTATE, MIRROR3D, 3DSCALE, SLICE, REVOLVE's 3D axis, CPLANE/UCS, CUTLIST `at`, PANEL from a profile (`base`, `at`), and plan-only commands run in a level plane that isn't World |
| Where their sources are | Results made from existing objects (EXTRUDE, REVOLVE, LOFT, SWEEP, PLANESURF from curves, PANEL from a profile, booleans, edge and face commands, SHELL, SLICE, PRESSPULL, the conversions and the mesh commands) and imports (STEPIN, IGESIN) |
| The world, picking faces and edges | FILLETEDGE, CHAMFEREDGE, SHELL, SOLIDEDIT and PRESSPULL take a face or edge as its index (the kernel's, see below) or as a point `[x,y,z]` on or next to it; SECTION, SECTIONPLANE, INTERFERE and FLATSHOT take world points and directions |

### Furniture

| Command | JSON form |
|---|---|
| PANEL | `{handle (closed profile) \| corner1: [x,y], corner2: [x,y], thickness? ### Faces and edges

The kernel names a solid's faces and edges by index, counting from 0 in OpenCASCADE's order of
the shape read from its exact B-rep text. Any edit makes new text with new indices, so JSON forms
usually name them by a point `[x,y,z]` on or next to them instead. At the commands' face and edge
prompts a click in a 3D view picks along the line of sight, a click in plan picks straight down,
and a typed point with a height picks the nearest face or edge. Picks stay on one solid per
operation, and every edit replaces the solid in place, keeping its layer, properties and part.

## Design

### Principles

- **The 2D pipeline stays.** Display lists, picking, snaps and the GPU canvas are the 2D ones. A
  view direction projects the plane of a planar object as a 2D affine transform, so circles,
  text, hatches and dimensions keep their 2D drawing code. Seen from straight above, everything
  draws exactly as 2D always has.
- **Objects live in 3D the AutoCAD way.** A planar object keeps 2D coordinates in its own plane
  (its OCS: the extrusion direction, DXF 210, raised by its elevation). Lines, 3D polylines,
  points, 3D faces, rays, helices and solids hold world points.
- **The document stays kernel-free.** A solid is data: the B-rep text, a cached tessellation and
  a transform. MOVE, ROTATE, SCALE, MIRROR and COPY change only the transform. The kernel is
  needed only to make or combine shapes.
- **Plan is AutoCAD, every other view is Rhino.** The UI switches on the view direction alone
  (`Xf::is_plan`). A plan view with the World plane runs the same code as plain 2D.
- **World is the identity.** Every construction-plane path returns early in World, so 2D work
  in World is untouched.

### Crash isolation

OpenCASCADE is C++. An uncaught exception aborts the process, and a hostile file can reach it.
So the apps register themselves as the kernel's worker: `cadcraft_kernel::worker_main()` runs
first in `main`, and every kernel operation then runs in a child process. That child is the app's
own executable started with `CADRHINO_KERNEL_WORKER=1`, one per operation, with a 60 s timeout.
It reads one JSON request on stdin and writes one marked reply line on stdout. A crash or timeout
becomes an error, and the command leaves the drawing as it was.

Opening a file meshes all its solids in one worker. If that worker dies, each solid is tried
alone, so the good ones still draw. B-rep text is checked for OpenCASCADE's header before it is
sent. Tests that register no worker run the kernel in-process; `crates/kernel/tests/worker.rs`
makes the test binary its own worker.

Without the `occt` feature (the web build), every operation returns "not available". Solids read
from a file then have no mesh and draw nothing, and a warning is logged for each.

### Building with the kernel (Windows, MSVC)

The desktop app and the CLI enable `cadcraft-kernel/occt` on native targets. The first build
compiles OpenCASCADE 7.8.1 from source into `target/OCCT`, which takes about 20 to 30 minutes.

1. CMake: Visual Studio's own works. Point `CMAKE` at it, for example
   `C:\Program Files\Microsoft Visual Studio\18\Community\Common7\IDE\CommonExtensions\Microsoft\CMake\CMake\bin\cmake.exe`.
   `.cargo/config.toml` sets `CMAKE_POLICY_VERSION_MINIMUM=3.5` for CMake 4.
2. Run `scripts/occt-msvc-setup.ps1` once before the first build, and again after OCCT is
   rebuilt. It adds the `opencascade-sys` junction that cxx-build needs without Developer Mode,
   and makes OCCT's `Handle_X` a typedef on MSVC, as the bindings expect.
3. `cargo test -p cadcraft-engine --features occt` runs the modelling tests;
   `cargo test -p cadcraft-kernel --features occt` runs the kernel's.

## Known ceilings

Deliberate shortcuts are marked `ponytail:` in the code with their upgrade path. This list
summarises the 3D ones on `main`.

### Views and projection

- A perspective view's display list is built for its camera, so panning and zooming it build it
  again (in the background: the last list shows meanwhile, true on the target plane). Curves are
  tessellated for the target plane's zoom, so near the eye they may show their facets.
- In perspective, filled TrueType text and clipped block contents in a plane the view direction
  lies in take that plane's depth at its line through the origin; lines without end run to their
  vanishing point; a raster image is a 16 × 16 grid of affine pieces, so one filling a
  wide-angle view bends a little at the cell edges; the gumball's handles point as the
  parallel view shows the axes; an XCLIP boundary reaching behind the eye loses those corners.
  Layouts and their viewports stay in plan. The 3D face snaps find a solid, mesh or 3D face under the
  cursor by its corners' box on screen, which is approximate for one reaching behind the eye.
- DVIEW has no slider drags, and the objects it asks for first don't stand in for the drawing (it
  all shows). Orbiting (3DORBIT, the ViewCube ring), VPOINT, PLAN and the preset views give an
  upright view without clipping planes. A line without ends square to the line of sight is kept
  or dropped whole by the clipping planes, and a solid cut open by one shows its inside, with no
  cap. Named views (VIEW) keep their twist but don't apply it when restored.
- The ViewCube follows the WCS, not the UCS, and has no rotate arrows in face views; dragging
  the cube itself doesn't orbit (the ring does).
- RENDER is not photoreal (see [Rendering](#rendering)): no shadows, reflections, textures,
  transparency or lights of the drawing's own; 3D faces and 2D objects aren't rendered, and Save
  Image... renders again on the UI thread. An XCLIPped block reference's solids are cut without
  caps, and past 50 million face-and-cell tests the rest of them render whole.
- Layout viewports show plan only. Shaded solids seen through them draw in painter order.
- -VIEW Orthographic points to the preset views. R2007+ visual style objects (DXF 348) are not
  read: a tile whose style is only there opens as wireframe.
- Inactive tiles show no rollover highlight or snap marker until clicked. Their display lists
  arrive one background build after the current tile's, and right after an edit a click there
  may only make the tile current. They are drawn without a depth buffer: their faces in painter
  order, their solid edges (Shaded with Edges, Hidden) tested against the faces' depths at the
  tile's resolution each time they are drawn.
- LIVESECTION only keeps and switches the flag: views don't cut solids at live section planes
  (that needs a clip plane per tile in the render crate and the canvas).
- The CPlane grid reaches one view's size around the centre. In a turned UCS the plan grid is
  never isometric.
- Seen exactly edge-on, 2D objects sit at the depth of the origin.

### Drawing and shading

- The CPU paths use painter order: inactive tiles, PNG export, plot preview, and the canvas
  without wgpu. Shaded faces are sorted per solid there, so interpenetrating solids can overlap
  wrongly, and lines draw over or under faces by draw order. The GPU canvas depth-tests; the
  raster and the CPU canvas depth-test the solids' edges.
- Hidden is the (unshaded, edges) pair rather than a style object. 3D faces and regions don't
  hide anything in Hidden. The Visual Styles Manager's styles are built in.
- The GPU depth buffer is 24-bit over the list's whole depth range, so very deep drawings lose
  resolution. Fills other than shaded faces only test depth. Edges are hairlines,
  except in named-view renders 1000 px or more on a side, where solid edges are 2 px wide.
- Thickness (DXF 39) is not drawn. Text and shapes in linetypes are not drawn along 3D lines.
- Meshing uses one deflection ratio (5e-4 of the shape's size) for every size.

### Construction planes and 2D under a UCS

- Plan-only commands run in World. In a turned UCS their own directions (horizontal and vertical
  constraints, a hatch's angle, Hor/Ver options) follow world X, and in a raised plane what they
  build from scratch sits at z = 0. A deferred tangent or perpendicular snap reaches them as its
  pick point.
- UCS takes an origin, 3 points and the presets only. The `*x,y` (WCS) prefix reads the same as
  the plain form.
- Dynamic-block grip drags work in level planes and are refused in tilted ones; a parameter's
  block z is ignored.
- The drawing as a plane sees it (`pick_doc`) is rebuilt whole after every change.
- Objects in tilted planes: they get no grip dimensions; a grip edit previews their shape flat
  on the CPlane; STRETCH moves one whole when its first grip is inside; TRIM, EXTEND, BREAK,
  FILLET, CHAMFER, JOIN and PEDIT refuse them. REFEDIT and NCOPY refuse references in tilted
  planes.
- Dimensions, leaders, multilines, multileaders, viewports, images, tables and tolerances stay
  level. A table runs along its direction (DXF group 11; one made in a turned UCS along the UCS's
  X axis) and keeps reading left to right when mirrored. Regions and wipeouts stay at z = 0, and
  new hatches and regions are made on the XY plane.
- QDIM's linear modes, like DIMLINEAR, run along world X and Y in a turned UCS; its Ordinate mode
  measures along the UCS's axes, as DIMORDINATE does.
- A non-planar DXF SPLINE is flattened. Derived splines (offsets, fits, joins) start at
  elevation 0. A hatch pattern under shear takes the average scale.
- Tilted block references: their extents and snaps use the contents' 2D box and transforms.
- A preview that can't be placed in the plane draws flat. A rubber band from a 3D-snapped point
  starts on the plane.

### Picking, snaps and the gumball

- 3D picking scans the whole display list.
- In 3D views and in plan with a tilted CPlane a click at an object prompt picks what is drawn.
  A prompt that takes a point to pick one object (OFFSET, FILLET, CHAMFER, PEDIT, LENGTHEN, BREAK,
  TRIM, EXTEND, DIMLINEAR …) gets the point of the object under the cursor, and that object is
  picked even where plan shows another (a shelf over the one below). Solids and rays there still
  come as a pick, which only PANEL, SWEEP and the select prompts take. Typed selection modes (W, C,
  F, WP, CP, BOX) clicked in such a view select what it draws in them; typed points select in
  CPlane coordinates, and so does STRETCH, whose windows mark the plane's area that moves.
- A grip dragged in its object's own plane (a circle standing square to the CPlane, say) sends
  the height of the point under the cursor in that plane with it, so the object stretches up and
  down too; grip ROTATE, SCALE and MIRROR stay in the plane.
- 3D snaps are END, MID, CEN, QUA, NOD, INS, GCEN, NEA, PER, TAN, INT, APP, EXT and PAR, and
  with the 3D object snaps on (F4, 3DOSMODE) Vertex, Midpoint on edge, Center of face, Knot,
  Perpendicular (to a face) and Nearest to face; OSNAPZ gives the point of the CPlane under the
  snap. APP is where objects cross on screen, not where their extensions would. EXT extends
  straight ends only; EXT and PAR acquire a line end or a line as soon as the cursor is over it
  and show their marker without a dotted path.
  A solid's face centres and exact edge midpoints come from the kernel in the background (a
  moment after the cursor first reaches it; until then the drawn edges' midpoints); a solid's
  spline edges have no knots; Perpendicular and Nearest to face use the drawn triangles (exact on
  flat faces). CEN on solids is for whole circles only; INT on an ellipse, spline or curved
  solid edge is within the tessellation (a quarter point on screen). Objects in block references
  aren't 3D-snapped. The candidates are rescanned as the cursor moves, and crossings (INT, APP)
  pair up at most the first 1,000 curve pieces under the cursor, so in a crowded zoomed-out view
  a crossing among the pieces left out isn't offered.
- The gumball's scale square doesn't snap. A block reference's box is its contents' plan box at
  its plane.
- A face or edge pick is a kernel call (about 0.1 s; the solids along the line of sight in one
  call): fine for a click, too slow to highlight what the cursor hovers over. Ctrl+click on a face
  or edge at a general Select objects prompt is not done.

### Modelling

- PYRAMID's prompts take the base centre, radius and height (the JSON form takes `sides` and
  `topRadius`).
- A ruled loft of more than two sections is pairwise lofts fused. LOFT has no POint, Join
  multiple edges, Normal to or Draft angle options, and MOde isn't remembered. With guides the
  sections need as many corners each, and the patches meet edge to edge, not smoothly across a
  guide or corner. A path and guides can't be combined (AutoCAD's are separate options too).
- SWEEP and POLYSOLID set the profile square to the path at its box centre, with mitred corners.
  POLYSOLID's preview is the path line; Arc has no Direction option; Justify isn't remembered.
- FILLETEDGE and CHAMFEREDGE on every edge refuse a radius or distance of half the solid's
  smallest box dimension or more; on chosen edges OpenCASCADE decides. CHAMFEREDGE's distances
  start at 1 each run, and FILLETRAD3D isn't saved to DXF. No Expression option.
- SOLIDEDIT: flat and curved faces move or turn separately; a curved face moves the feature it
  bounds (one bounding none can't move), and a flat face moved within its plane stays put. Face
  and edge colours and materials, Extrude Path, Rotate Axis by object and Reference, and the
  2points default aren't available. Clean also removes imprinted edges on flat faces.
- PRESSPULL has no live preview while dragging; a bounded area is one closed object (not a
  boundary found from several curves, as HATCH does). A closed curve on its own is extruded
  without its holes.
- XEDGES does 3D solids and surfaces only. THICKEN and PRESSPULL make solids of surfaces; a
  surface whose faces aren't all picked is refused.
- CONVTOSOLID converts each surface on its own (AutoCAD's SURFSCULPT joins several). A converted
  mesh is one flat face per triangle (merged where flat); MAX_MESH_TRIS is 50,000.
- 3DPRINT writes the STL in drawing units (no 3D Print Options).
- Ellipses and splines go to the kernel sampled and refitted.
- The kernel takes similarity transforms only.

### Furniture

- PANEL from a plan profile uses its x and y only.
- CUTLIST's smallest-rectangle search tries at most 512 outline edges. A free-form part's exact
  box comes from a fine mesh (1e-4 of its size): its first measurement takes most of a second
  (about 1 s for a lofted seat in a debug build). The box is then kept for the session, also
  after the part moves, so a cut list made again takes milliseconds; a turned or edited part is
  measured again.
- VIEWBASE's hidden lines are sampled over a capped grid, and curved edges come out as
  polylines. Without the kernel's outlines it guesses creases at 40°. Its dimensions are overall
  sizes only, and the iso view shows no hidden lines. SOLVIEW and SOLDRAW run VIEWBASE: AutoCAD's
  Ucs/Ortho/Auxiliary/Section viewports aren't built.
- SECTION makes polylines, not regions (a region can't leave z = 0); ellipse and spline cuts
  become polylines through points. SECTIONPLANE has no face pick, jogged section or Type, and a
  copied plane keeps its name. Section and FLATSHOT blocks are named SECTION1…, FLATSHOT1…
- FLATSHOT leaves out smooth (tangent) edges except as silhouettes. Hidden lines use the
  drawing's HIDDEN linetype.
- INTERFERE has no Nested selection, Settings or zoom to each pair.

### Exchange

- IGES: objects inside block references are not exported. Files are written in millimetres, and
  ellipses go out as splines.
- IGES import groups faces from the B-rep text with one matching grid per file, without sewing.
  Voids are matched to lumps by bounding boxes.
- Only the B-rep header is checked before the kernel parses it. Crafted data can crash the
  worker, not the app.

### Kernel process

- One worker process per operation (about 0.1 s each), stopped after 60 s. SECTION,
  SECTIONPLANETOBLOCK and face and edge picks send all their solids in one call. A long-lived
  worker, restarted after a crash, is the upgrade path if many solids get slow. Exact boxes (CUTLIST,
  VIEWBASE) are cached for the session, at most 20,000, and the first measurement of several
  parts is spread across up to 4 workers.
